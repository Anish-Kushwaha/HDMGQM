Covariant mathematical formulation

Physics & Mathematics

The framework is organized around one variational structure, one total stress-energy tensor and a self-consistent axisymmetric Einstein-Maxwell-matter problem.

1. Units and invariants

Heaviside-Lorentz natural units are used: ℏ = c = kB = ε0 = μ0 = 1, with metric signature (−,+,+,+).

F = ¼ FμνFμν    G = ¼ FμνF̃μν

For a purely magnetic field in the comoving frame, F = B²/2 and G = 0.

2. Defining action

S = ∫ d⁴x √(−g) [ R/(16πG) − ¼ FμνFμν + ΔLHE(F,G) + Lmatter ]

The dense-matter sector is modular. The framework permits a relativistic mean-field model, chiral-EFT matched closure, tabulated microscopic EOS, or controlled quark-matter closure, while the coupling and stress-accounting architecture remains fixed.

Action architecture — the anti-double-counting rule of Volume 3.

3. Field equations & conservation

Einstein

Gμν = 8πG Tμν

Electromagnetism

∇μHμν = Jν
∇[αFβγ] = 0

Conservation

∇μTμν = 0

Electromagnetic and magnetization forces are already encoded in the total variational stress accounting; the same contribution must not be inserted twice.

4. Landau quantization

Eiν(pz) = √[ pz² + (m*i)² + 2ν|qi|B ]
gν = 2 − δν0
kF,iν = √[ (μ*i)² − (m*i)² − 2ν|qi|B ]
νmax,i = floor{ [ (μ*i)² − (m*i)² ] / [2|qi|B] }

At zero temperature the charged-particle density is a discrete sum over occupied levels. The lowest-Landau-level condition is species-specific; neutral species are not Landau-quantized through minimal electric coupling, although anomalous magnetic moments can still couple them to B.

Landau-level ladder: transverse motion is quantized while longitudinal momentum remains continuous.

5. Grand potential, magnetization & pressures

Ω = Ωm + ΩrenHE
M = −(∂Ω/∂B){μᵢ},T
ε = Ω + Σᵢ μᵢ nᵢ + B²/2
P∥ = −Ω − B²/2
P⊥ = −Ω − MB + B²/2
ΔP = P⊥ − P∥ = B² − MB

The numerical implementation must preserve one internally consistent convention for ensemble, vacuum contribution and field stress.

One grand potential generates number density, magnetization, energy density and principal stresses.

6. Covariant anisotropic stress tensor

Tμν = εuμuν + P⊥hμν + (P∥ − P⊥)bμbν
hμν = gμν + uμuν

With u·u = −1, b·b = 1 and u·b = 0, the anisotropy is geometric rather than a coordinate artifact. The magnetic axis selects one principal direction; isotropy is recovered when P∥ = P⊥.

7. Axisymmetric equilibrium

ds² = −N²dt² + A²(dr² + r²dθ²) + C²r²sin²θ(dφ − ωdt)²

N, A, C and ω depend on (r,θ). A nonrotating model sets ω = 0. The electromagnetic four-potential and current prescription define the magnetic topology and boundary-value problem.

Gμν = 8πGTμν
∇μHμν = Jν
∇[αFβγ] = 0
∇μTμν = 0
Equilibrium is a coupled nonlinear boundary-value problem, not a scalar pressure correction.

8. Stability hierarchy

Local

Thermodynamic consistency, compressibility, susceptibility and characteristic-speed constraints.

Global

Sequence diagnostics in M, Mb, J, EB, ρc, radii, quadrupole and magnetic ellipticity.

Dynamical

Linearized coupled equations and eigenfrequencies; instability corresponds to Im(ω) > 0 under the adopted convention.

Local thermodynamics → global equilibrium → perturbation spectrum.
